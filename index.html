Exercise 1: Hiring pack in 3 minutes

10 minAny tool

Warm-upOne minute, any tool. Make this message polite.

  • Rewrite this message politely and professionally for staff: “Many people coming late. Please be on time.”
Prompt
You are a senior talent acquisition lead at a pharmaceutical plant in Verna, Goa. Create a hiring pack for a Production Supervisor (oral solid dosage, 4–7 years' experience, managing 25 operators across 3 shifts). Include:
1) a job description under 250 words,
2) 8 interview questions (4 technical, 4 behavioural),
3) a scoring sheet with 5 criteria rated 1–5, describing what a 5 looks like.
Use simple Indian English.

Then askTurn the pack into a document you can edit.

  • Give me this as a Word file.

Try yoursSwap in “Front Office Manager, 5-star resort in Candolim”, or a role you are hiring for right now.

Good to knowCandidates now use AI to write CVs packed with the keywords from your job description. Screen for evidence of what someone has actually done, not for keyword matches.

Prompt
Attached are 60 exit-interview comments from our plant. Some are in Hinglish and Hindi.
1) Group them into 5–7 themes and count the comments in each.
2) For each theme, give 2 representative quotes, translated to English where needed.

Follow upAsk the next question below in the same chat.

Exercise 5: Policy chatbot in 5 minutes

10 minNotebookLMClaudeChatGPT
AttachPDFCureAll_Leave_and_Attendance_Policy.pdfPDFCureAll_POSH_Policy.pdfPDFCureAll_Travel_and_Expense_Policy.pdf
  • NotebookLM: create a notebook and add the 3 PDFs as sources.
  • Claude or ChatGPT: create a Project, add the 3 PDFs, and paste these instructions.
Project instructions
You are the HR helpdesk for CureAll Pharma. Answer only from the attached policies. Name the policy and section for every answer. If the policies do not say, reply "Please check with Plant HR" and never guess. Keep answers under 80 words, in simple English.

Ask itFive questions, including two that test whether it admits what it does not know.

  1. I have 18 days of PL left on 31 March. What happens to them?Watch: the policy does not say what happens to the 3 days above the 15-day carry-forward. A good bot says so.
  2. I am an MR. I travel from Hubballi to Belagavi and back the same day. What allowance do I get?
  3. A colleague keeps sending me jokes on WhatsApp that make me uncomfortable. What can I do, and by when?
  4. Do I get paternity leave if we adopt a child?
  5. How many work-from-home days do I get?Trap: no policy covers this.

If it inventsThat is a hallucination: an answer that sounds right but is wrong or invented. Ask it to name the policy section, and check before you act on anything about a person. More in When AI makes things up.

BonusTurn the leave policy into something new joiners can listen to or watch. In the same NotebookLM notebook, open Studio and create an Audio Overview (two AI hosts talk it through, like a podcast) and a Video Overview (narrated slides). Both can be made in Hindi, Marathi or Konkani: pick the language before you create them.

Paste in the Customise box
Explain the leave policy to new shop-floor joiners: the types of leave, how to apply, how much can be carried forward, and what to do if you are absent without notice. Keep it simple and practical.

CheckListen or watch before you share it. It can still get a detail wrong.

Safe use

Three things to keep in mind before you use AI on real HR work.

When AI makes things up

AI can give an answer that sounds right but is wrong or invented. This is called a hallucination.

Why it happens

  • It fills gaps when it lacks information
  • It guesses when the prompt is unclear
  • It sounds just as confident when it is wrong

How to avoid it

  • Ask it to name its source or policy section
  • Use thinking or deep research mode for important questions
  • Check with a trusted source or a colleague
  • Never act on unchecked output about people

AI, human, or both?

For each situation, decide who should do it. Then check the answer.

  • AI can do it
  • Human: a person decides
  • Both: AI drafts, you check
  • Neither: don't do it
  1. A Diwali greeting to all employees

    Both. AI drafts; you check the names and the tone.

  2. Deciding whether to terminate an employee

    Human. A person decides. AI can help you prepare the facts.

  3. Summarising 60 exit-interview comments

    Both. Remove names first, then check the themes against the comments.

  4. Pasting the salary sheet, with names, into a free AI tool

    Neither. Don't do it.

The sweet spot is usually both: AI drafts, you decide.

Never paste into an AI tool

Anonymise first: replace names with IDs and remove contact details, the way the practice files already are.

  • Names together with salaries, ratings or disciplinary details
  • Aadhaar, PAN, UAN or bank account numbers
  • Medical information, or the details of a POSH complaint
  • Anything confidential or not yet announced: restructuring, layoffs, acquisitions

Quick test: would you be okay if everyone saw this? If not, don't paste it.

Generally safe to share

  • Fictional examples
  • Rough notes
  • Process steps without names

Five rules for AI at work

  1. Use your company's approved AI tool for real company data. Ask IT which one that is.
  2. On personal accounts, check the privacy setting that decides whether your chats train the model.
  3. Check every number and every legal statement. AI can be confidently wrong.
  4. AI drafts, you decide. Read every line of an offer, warning or termination letter before it goes out.
  5. When a result affects someone's job, keep a person in the loop and a record of how the decision was made.
